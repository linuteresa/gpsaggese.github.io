Milestone 2: Counterfactual blame profiles in chess endgames

Results completed on November 19, 2026.

Summary

We completed the planned chess feasibility study: 3,900 continuations covering every decision in seven naturally failed runs, followed by a full replay and billing audit. Three original replies showed harmful effects after correcting for all 48 primary decision tests. In the strongest case, re-deciding the reply increased wins from 5/30 to 25/30, a gain of 66.7 percentage points.

The results show that restarting from a saved decision boundary can identify consequential mistakes in real agent trajectories. They cover four starting positions and support a feasibility conclusion; they do not establish a general failure taxonomy or complete Milestones 3 and 4.

Study design

The inputs were the ten thinking-enabled pilot games from the chess Milestone 1 submission, pinned at commit 8799acfc484febd0c9156ccca0bfa00fb0724aeb. Three games succeeded and seven failed. The failed games supplied 48 decision boundaries across four king-and-rook-versus-king starting positions.

At each boundary, we restored the saved conversation, board and remaining-call limit. The keep arm forced the original complete assistant reply and then resumed normal play. The re-decide arm sampled a new reply and continued under the same policy. Each primary decision received 30 fresh continuations per arm. A step includes the reply and execution of its command, so the measured effect applies to the whole reply rather than the move alone.

Repair gain is P(win | re-decide) − P(win | keep original reply). Positive gain means retrying helps; negative gain means the original reply helped. The policy remained Claude Haiku 5.5 with adaptive thinking, a 16,000-token output limit, text-only reply history, the original absolute step limits and the same deterministic defender.

The primary profiles used 2,880 continuations. Two complete profiles were repeated independently with another 1,020 continuations, for 3,900 in total. These samples were independent of the original Milestone 1 outcomes and the earlier 300-continuation validation stage. Fixed sample counts were retained throughout collection.

The initial checkpoint-value shortcut could not be validated precisely enough at the proposed sample sizes. The prospectively declared direct-arm fallback was therefore used for all causal labels. We report Wilson 95% intervals for win probabilities and Newcombe 95% intervals for gains. Intervals are unadjusted; two-sided Fisher tests use Holm correction across the 48 primary decisions.

At krk-m4-1, trial 0, step 3, the original reply plays Ke2 and assumes that Black can only respond Kg1. In fact, Ke2 blocks the white rook on d2, leaving Kh2, Kg2 and Kg1 legal. The recorded defender chooses Kg2. The position remains theoretically winnable under the clock-aware oracle, but keeping this reply wins only 5/30 continuations, compared with 25/30 when re-deciding. This is a policy-specific effect that the oracle value alone does not reveal.

At trial 0, step 5, the reply plays Rd1+. After Black responds Kg2, the halfmove clock advances from 93 to 95 and the clock-aware diagnostic changes from winnable to not winnable. The keep arm has no wins, while re-deciding wins 11/30.

At trial 1, step 1, the reply plays Rh3 and claims the rook cannot be captured. Black immediately takes it with Kxh3, leaving king versus king and an insufficient-material draw. Re-deciding wins 11/30. All three cases are natural recorded failures, not planted blunders.

The other 45 primary decisions remain unresolved after multiplicity correction. This does not show that their true effects are zero. No helpful decision survives correction across the primary family. The helpful point estimate at krk-m4-1, trial 0, step 1 is −33.3 percentage points, consistent with −32.0 points in the earlier independent validation stage, but its primary Holm-adjusted test is not significant.

The krk-m4-1 profiles provide evidence of localized harmful decisions: steps 3 and 5 in one run and the first step in the other. The repeated profiles broadly preserve the ordering of stronger and weaker decision effects, but many late gains are zero in both samples. Those flat tails contribute substantially to rank agreement and low average differences. Early-step gains differ by as much as 20 percentage points, so correlation and overlapping intervals do not prove equality of effects. The predeclared repeats covered krk-m3-1 and krk-m6-1; the large krk-m4-1 effects were not independently repeated in this phase.

Technical verification and cost

The complete-population audit verified 16,165 paid model calls, 18,115 recorded decision steps, 20,995 content-addressed blobs and 3,916 stored branch segments for 3,900 completed samples. Branch parents, snapshot identities, API-visible input hashes, model settings, remaining-call limits, command outputs, legal games, independent referee outcomes and request-level costs all reconciled. All 30 applicable tests passed. Original input hashes and pinned executable source hashes remained unchanged; a historical hash-check exception was limited to a mutable pytest cache file.

An administrative interruption after 1,414 completed samples was handled by retaining all completed outcomes and continuing the 16 interrupted trajectories from their last saved checkpoints. No sampled prefix was redrawn.

Data prepared for Milestone 4

The export contains 48 decision rows with 28 numerical features derived only from the saved pre-decision board and conversation. Primary gain labels and uncertainty are stored separately. Oracle diagnostics, future outcomes and the input-timing audit are excluded from predictor inputs. Task and difficulty-bin identifiers are grouping metadata, and all trials and descendants of a starting position must remain in one train/evaluation split.

A concrete timing limitation appears in krk-m4-1: the two first decisions have identical saved context and board, but different original replies with gain estimates of −0.333 and +0.367. Their pre-generation feature vectors are identical. A state-only predictor cannot distinguish the effects of those candidate replies. Reply-specific prediction will require an explicitly declared feature point after candidate generation and before execution, with no future outcomes used as inputs.

Conclusion and limits

The approved Milestone 2 chess feasibility experiment is complete. It produces direct decision-level gain estimates, uncertainty intervals, seven natural-failure profiles, two independent repeated profiles and an audited feature/label export. The strongest finding is that retrying selected replies can substantially improve outcomes even when the board remains theoretically winnable after the original action.

The evidence is limited to seven selected failures from four positions. It does not establish population-wide failure frequencies, cross-model or cross-family transfer, a validated surrogate, or a scaffold improvement. Fresh model generation has no seed in this protocol; exact environment replay is distinct from model determinism, and no immutable provider weight digest is recorded. Further work should expand the task groups, independently confirm the discovery profiles and define candidate-reply feature timing before Milestone 4 training.
